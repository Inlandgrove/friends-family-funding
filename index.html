Who We're For

We call our customers "Pros."

Not because they play professionally. Because they carry themselves like it. They don't need gimmicks. They don't need to fit in. They show up as themselves — on the course and off it.

Golf is already a mental game. The last thing you need is to feel like you're wearing a costume while you play it. Nobody feels like themselves wearing a hot dog print polo on the course.

We're for the Pros who go to the course for fun — but not the kind of fun that involves Fireball shots and harassing the cart girl. We're for the ones who appreciate the scenery. The camaraderie. The peace. The four hours where the phone stays in the bag and the world gets quiet.

We're for the ones who bring their partner to the course — who see golf as something to share, not escape into.

We're not for everyone. We know that. And we confidently accept it.

The Brand

Not a golf brand. A fashion brand inspired by golf.

That's the shift. We're not selling polos with logos. We're giving our Pros the feeling that they belong on this course — on their own terms.

Inland Grove was born at the intersection of golf heritage and street culture. Our name pays homage to growing up in the Inland Empire, California — orange groves, views of Big Bear Mountain, and municipal courses at golden hour.

Hollister was built around surfing.
Inland Grove will be built on golf.

The Story

I got lost for a while.

The first two years I spent chasing what I thought a golf brand was supposed to look like. Somewhere along the way I forgot why I started this in the first place.

"Where Culture Meets the Course" — that's not marketing. That's me.

Here's what nobody tells you about this business: you're only as good as your ad spend. I haven't spent a dollar. Everything you see — $136K in sales — came from popups, trade shows, and me showing up. Nights. Weekends. While working a full-time job in tech.

This is what I built in the margins. Imagine what happens when I can actually focus.

I know what this brand is now. I know who it's for. And for the first time, I'm not doing it alone — I've got a team that can actually execute.

I've never been good at asking for help. Not because of pride — because I was always more scared of how I'd feel about the people who said no. I've spent my whole life trying to help everyone around me. This time I'm focused on myself.

I've spent years showing up for everyone else. This is me showing up for myself — and asking the people I trust to show up with me.

Questions

What you're probably wondering

What happens if the business fails?

This is a bet on me and the brand. If Inland Grove doesn't generate revenue, payments may be delayed or reduced. There's no guarantee. Only invest what you're comfortable with.

Is this a loan or an investment?

Neither in the traditional sense. It's a revenue share agreement. You're not buying equity in the company, and it's not a loan with fixed payments. You get paid back as a percentage of revenue until you hit your return cap.

Why wait until June 2027 to start payments?

The money goes directly into growth — marketing, inventory, trade shows. We need time to deploy that capital and see the revenue impact before we start paying it back. This timeline gives us room to execute without strangling the business early.

What if multiple people invest?

Each investor gets their percentage of monthly revenue until they hit their cap. Those percentages stack — which is why we're raising a specific amount and being thoughtful about the structure.

What if you sell the company before I'm paid back?

If we exit before investors are fully paid, we'll pay out remaining balances from the proceeds. You won't be left hanging.

Do I need to report this on my taxes?

The payments you receive are likely taxable income. We'll provide documentation at the end of each tax year. Consult your tax advisor for your specific situation.

Can I invest more than $25,000?

Reach out directly. We can talk.
